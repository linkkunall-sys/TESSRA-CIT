4.  ROLES  AND  PRIORITY  The  platform  should  have  four  roles.  Nobody  can  approve  their  own  request.  
Role  What  they  can  do  
Admin  (Dean's  office)  Everything.  Generates  the  timetable,  approves  timetable  changes  and  any  booking  that  moves  a  class,  can  change  the  priority  order.  
Department  coordinator  Requests  changes  to  their  department's  sessions,  books  rooms  for  department  events,  approves  bookings  of  their  department's  labs.  
Faculty  Views  their  own  timetable,  marks  unavailability,  requests  extra  or  moved  sessions.  
Club  representative  Submits  and  cancels  venue  requests,  and  sees  the  status  and  reason  for  each.  
When  two  requests  want  the  same  room  at  the  same  time,  this  order  decides  who  gets  it:

8.  JUDGING  CRITERIA  Criterion  Marks  How  it's  judged  
Scheduling  correctness  25  Validator  run  on  your  exports/schedule.json  and  on  the  timetable  the  judges  generate  in  your  app  (timetable,  bookings  and  exams).  25  marks  minus  3  for  every  hard  constraint  violation,  minimum  0.  
Rescheduling  15  Judges  apply  their  own  change  file  in  your  app.  6  for  no  hard  violations  after  the  change,  5  for  moving  the  fewest  sessions  compared  to  other  teams,  4  for  a  clear  view  of  the  changes  and  a  proper  approval  step.  
Optimisation  10  Soft  penalty  from  the  validator,  compared  across  teams  that  scored  at  least  15  in  correctness.  
Use  of  AI  15  Judges  try  the  same  set  of  tricky  requests  on  every  app:  vague  timings,  conditions,  priority  abuse,  Hinglish,  attempts  to  get  around  the  rules.  
Booking  decisions  10  Booking  score  from  the  validator,  and  whether  each  rejection  comes  with  a  reason  that  makes  sense.  
Usability  and  dashboard  10  Can  a  judge  who  has  never  seen  your  app  find  a  room,  read  a  timetable  and  approve  a  change,  using  only  the  README  and  the  demo  accounts?  
UI  and  design  10  Look  and  feel  of  the  frontend:  consistent  layout  and  styling,  timetable  grids  that  are  easy  to  read,  works  on  both  laptop  and  phone  screens,  proper  loading,  empty  and  error  states,  and  overall  polish.  Judged  on  the  live  app,  not  just  the  video.  
Code  and  documentation  5  Setup  works  from  the  README,  the  code  is  reasonably  organised,  and  the  AI  and  third-party  declarations  are  complete.
